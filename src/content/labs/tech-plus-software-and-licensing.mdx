## Software Asset Management: Legality, Architecture, and Cost

Software is intellectual property. When an organization buys software, it almost never buys the program itself — it purchases a **License (End User License Agreement / EULA)** granting permission to execute the software under strict conditions.

In enterprise IT, Software Asset Management (SAM) is a high-stakes responsibility:
1. **Compliance:** Deploying more copies than you have licensed exposes your organization to severe civil fines and legal audits.
2. **Cost Optimization:** Choosing between perpetual licenses, SaaS subscriptions, and concurrent floating pools can save tens of thousands of dollars.
3. **Open Source Governance:** Ensuring that development projects adhere to GPL, MIT, and Apache open-source obligations.

<Callout type="exam">
CompTIA Tech+ FC0-U71 tests the characteristics of software licensing models: Proprietary (closed-source commercial), Open Source (freely inspectable/modifiable code), Freeware (zero-cost closed source), Subscription/SaaS (cloud-hosted recurring fee), and Concurrent Licensing (pooled active seats).
</Callout>

<Callout type="warn">
Never assume that software running in an educational or non-profit building is exempt from copyright enforcement. Software vendors conduct rigorous licensing audits on school districts. An unauthorized "crack" or single-user key cloned across a classroom will fail automated audit scans and result in immediate license forfeiture and back-licensing penalties.
</Callout>
