steps:
  - id: cia-map
    title: Classify the impact
    prompt: A misconfigured file share has made student records readable by anyone on the internet. The data is intact and the share is still online. Which part of the CIA triad has failed?
    inputLabel: Primary CIA category
    hint: Ask what was actually lost, not what could happen next.
    validator:
      type: choice
      options:
        - "Confidentiality — data was disclosed to people who should not see it"
        - "Integrity — data was altered without authorisation"
        - "Availability — data could not be reached when needed"
        - "All three failed equally"
      correctIndex: 0
      rationale: "Nothing was changed, so integrity holds. The share is still reachable, so availability holds. The single failure is disclosure to unauthorised parties, which is confidentiality. Naming the specific failure drives which controls you reach for."
    successMessage: Correct. Exposure without alteration or downtime is a confidentiality failure.
  - id: risk-priority
    title: Prioritise the risk
    prompt: The exposed records contain student names, dates of birth, and home addresses, and the share is reachable from the public internet. How should this be prioritised?
    inputLabel: Risk level
    validator:
      type: choice
      options:
        - "Critical — regulated personal data is exposed to an unbounded audience"
        - "Low — no evidence anyone has accessed it yet"
        - "Medium — it can wait for the next scheduled maintenance window"
        - "Informational — log it for the quarterly review"
      correctIndex: 0
      rationale: "Risk combines impact with likelihood of exposure. Minors' PII carries legal and safeguarding obligations, and internet-facing means the potential audience is everyone. Absence of evidence of access is not evidence of no access — with no logging you simply cannot tell, which argues for urgency, not comfort."
    successMessage: Correct. Regulated PII plus unbounded exposure is a top-priority incident.
  - id: first-control
    title: First control action
    prompt: Which action should you take first to reduce the blast radius?
    inputLabel: Immediate control
    hint: Containment comes before investigation and before permanent redesign.
    validator:
      type: choice
      options:
        - "Remove public access from the share so only authorised accounts can reach it"
        - "Draft an incident report for leadership"
        - "Rebuild the file server with a hardened configuration"
        - "Email all affected students to warn them"
      correctIndex: 0
      rationale: "Stop the bleeding first: revoking public access ends the exposure in seconds. Reporting and notification matter and are often legally required, but they do not close the hole. Rebuilding the server is the right long-term fix and the slowest possible containment."
    successMessage: Correct. Containment first, then investigation, then permanent fixes.
  - id: layered-defense
    title: Defense-in-depth check
    prompt: Access is now restricted. Which additional layer would most improve your ability to detect a repeat of this incident?
    inputLabel: Additional layer
    validator:
      type: choice
      options:
        - "Access logging and alerting on the share"
        - "A longer minimum password length"
        - "A second firewall in front of the existing one"
        - "Annual security awareness training"
      correctIndex: 0
      rationale: "The reason you could not answer 'has anyone read this?' is that nothing was recording access. Logging and alerting close that gap directly. The other three are all legitimate controls, but none of them would tell you that a share had been exposed or who reached it."
    successMessage: Correct. Detection is the layer that was missing here.
  - id: closure-note
    title: Analyst closure note
    prompt: Write one line containing both words "contained" and "monitored".
    inputLabel: Closure note
    placeholder: "ex: Contained public exposure and monitored for repeat access attempts."
    validator:
      type: regex
      pattern: '^(?=.*\bcontained\b)(?=.*\bmonitored\b).+$'
      flags: "i"
    successMessage: Excellent. Your closure note reflects both action and follow-through.
checklist:
  - Incident mapped to correct CIA category
  - Risk level prioritized before mitigation
  - Immediate containment control selected
  - Additional defense layer identified
  - Closure note documents containment and monitoring
hints:
  - Choose controls that can be executed immediately.
  - Treat high-impact data exposure as urgent.
  - Always pair a fix with monitoring.

This lab turns CIA triad theory into a repeatable incident prioritization workflow.

## Completion Standard

Pass all checks and submit a closure note that proves the incident was contained and monitored.
