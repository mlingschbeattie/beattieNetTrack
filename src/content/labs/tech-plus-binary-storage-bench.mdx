## Triage: The 931 GB Mystery

Every computer technician has had this conversation:

A customer buys a 1 Terabyte SSD, plugs it into Windows 11, and immediately calls the help desk angry because Windows Disk Management reports **931 GB**. They believe they were short-changed 69 gigabytes of storage or sold a counterfeit drive.

In this lab, you work this ticket from end to end:
1. Converting raw binary and decimal values.
2. Querying live hardware telemetry via PowerShell.
3. Reading an authentic Windows 11 Disk Management console render.
4. Performing the mathematical conversion between Base-10 (decimal) and Base-2 (binary).
5. Documenting the resolution in a customer-facing closure note.

<Callout type="exam">
CompTIA Tech+ FC0-U71 heavily tests the distinction between **storage capacity** (measured in bytes: KB, MB, GB, TB, PB) and **network transfer speed** (measured in bits per second: Kbps, Mbps, Gbps). Always check whether the letter **B** is uppercase (Bytes) or lowercase (bits). 1 Byte = 8 bits.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warn">
Never tell a customer their drive is defective or attempt to reformat a drive to "reclaim" the 69 GB gap. Reformatting will never change this number — the gap is an arithmetic conversion artifact caused by Windows using binary powers of 1024 while displaying decimal "GB" labels.
</Callout>
