## Hardware Engineering: Matching Silicon to Workloads

In enterprise IT, anyone can take a credit card and buy the most expensive PC on a gaming website. That is not engineering — that is reckless spending.

A true systems technician analyzes the exact workload demands:
1. **Memory:** Sizing capacity and verifying multi-channel memory bus width.
2. **Expansion:** Mapping processor PCI Express lane budgets so high-speed storage and GPUs do not bottleneck each other.
3. **Power:** Calculating component electrical wattage and applying a 20% safety margin.
4. **Display:** Matching panel technologies (IPS vs TN vs OLED) and interface standards (DisplayPort MST vs HDMI).
5. **Thermals:** Matching CPU TDP envelopes to active cooling solutions.

<Callout type="exam">
CompTIA Tech+ FC0-U71 tests the physical and operational parameters of workstation components: RAM multi-channel doubling, PCIe lane allocation (x1, x4, x8, x16), modular PSU power distribution, and DisplayPort MST daisy-chaining.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warn">
Never size a power supply to match only the exact running wattage of your components. Power supplies operate with maximum electrical efficiency and longevity between 50% and 80% load. Furthermore, modern high-end GPUs experience microsecond "transient power spikes" that can pull 100W+ over their rated TDP. A PSU without at least 20% headroom will trigger emergency Over-Current Protection (OCP) shutoffs mid-render.
</Callout>
