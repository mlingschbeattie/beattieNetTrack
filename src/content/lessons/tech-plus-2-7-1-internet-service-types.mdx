## Internet Service Types

The technology used to deliver internet service to a home or business determines speed, reliability, latency, and availability. As a technician, you'll need to explain these options to users and troubleshoot connectivity issues specific to each type.

### Cable Internet

- Delivered over the same coaxial cable network used for cable TV
- **DOCSIS** standard (Data Over Cable Service Interface Specification)
- **Speeds:** 100 Mbps – 1+ Gbps downstream; asymmetric (upload slower than download)
- **Shared bandwidth** with neighbors on the same cable segment — congestion during peak hours
- Widely available in urban/suburban areas

### DSL (Digital Subscriber Line)

- Delivered over existing telephone copper wire pairs
- **ADSL** — Asymmetric DSL (faster download than upload); most residential
- **VDSL** — Very-high-speed DSL; faster but requires proximity to DSLAM
- **Speeds:** 1–100 Mbps (ADSL–VDSL2); distance-sensitive (farther from central office = slower)
- Advantage: Dedicated line per user (not shared like cable)

### Fiber Optic

- Light pulses through glass or plastic fiber — no electrical interference
- **FTTH/FTTP** — Fiber to the Home/Premises: fiber runs all the way to the building
- **FTTC** — Fiber to the Curb: fiber to a node; copper for last stretch
- **Speeds:** 100 Mbps – 10+ Gbps; symmetric (upload = download)
- Lowest latency; most reliable; limited geographic availability

### Satellite Internet

- Signal bounces from ground to satellite in orbit and back
- **Traditional (GEO):** 22,236 miles altitude; 600ms+ latency; Hughesnet, Viasat
- **LEO (Low Earth Orbit):** 340–1,200 miles altitude; 20–40ms latency; Starlink
- Useful in rural areas with no cable/fiber/DSL infrastructure
- Weather can degrade signal

### Cellular / Mobile Broadband

- Uses the same cell towers as mobile phones (4G LTE, 5G)
- **4G LTE:** 10–50+ Mbps typical; ubiquitous
- **5G:** Sub-6 GHz (50–400 Mbps); mmWave (1+ Gbps but short range)
- Hot-spots and cellular routers allow sharing mobile data to multiple devices
- Data caps and throttling common on consumer plans

### Fixed Wireless Access (FWA)

- Antenna at premises receives signal from a tower line-of-sight
- Avoids digging trenches for cable/fiber — good for rural deployments
- **Speeds:** 25–1,000+ Mbps depending on technology
- Requires clear line of sight to tower

### Comparison Summary

| Type | Speed Range | Latency | Availability | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cable | 100M–1G | Low | Urban/suburban | Shared segment |
| DSL | 1–100 Mbps | Low | Anywhere phone lines exist | Distance-sensitive |
| Fiber | 100M–10G | Very low | Limited | Best option when available |
| Satellite (GEO) | 25–100 Mbps | Very high | Rural | Weather-sensitive |
| Satellite (LEO) | 50–200 Mbps | Low | Growing | Starlink |
| Cellular | 10–400 Mbps | Low–medium | Broad | Data caps apply |

### Key Exam Points

- Fiber has the **lowest latency** and **highest speed** potential
- DSL speed degrades with **distance** from the central office
- GEO satellite has very **high latency** (~600ms) — bad for gaming/VoIP
- Cable uses **DOCSIS** standard; bandwidth is shared on the local segment
