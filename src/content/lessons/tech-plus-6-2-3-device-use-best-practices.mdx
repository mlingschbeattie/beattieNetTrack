## Security is a Daily Muscle Memory

You can install biometric retina scanners on the front doors and spend millions on cybersecurity firewalls.

Yet, every single day, massive data breaches occur because:
- An accountant stepped away to get a cup of coffee and left their computer unlocked (**Windows + L** ignored).
- A manager wrote their administrative password on a yellow sticky note stuck to the bottom of their monitor.
- An employee found a random USB flash drive in the elevator and plugged it in to see who it belonged to.

**Physical and Operational Security Hygiene** is not about software code—it is about daily, disciplined human habits that prevent the most common security mistakes in the workplace.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        THE DAILY SECURITY HYGIENE TRIAD                │
├────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **1. Screen Lock** │ Instantly lock your screen (`Win + L`) every time │
│                    │ you stand up from your chair. No exceptions.      │
├────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **2. Clean Desk**  │ Lock printed PII and medical charts in drawers.   │
│                    │ Never leave passwords on sticky notes!            │
├────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **3. USB Caution** │ Never plug an unknown USB drive into any machine. │
│                    │ Encrypt all approved external storage drives.     │
└────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 1. The Acceptable Use Policy (AUP)

When you join a school, hospital, or corporation, the first document IT hands you is the **Acceptable Use Policy (AUP)**:
- **Corporate Property Notice:** All computers, monitors, networks, and emails belong to the organization. Users have no expectation of personal privacy on corporate equipment.
- **Prohibited Actions:** No installing pirated software, no attempting to bypass network web filters (e.g. using unauthorized VPNs), and no storing illegal or copyright-infringing media.
- **Incident Reporting:** Mandatory obligation to immediately notify IT if a laptop is lost, stolen, or exhibits signs of malware infection.

## 2. Screen Locking: The 1-Second Habit

Leaving an unattended workstation logged in is the equivalent of leaving your front door wide open with the keys in the lock.

Any passerby—a disgruntled coworker, a rogue cleaning contractor, or an unauthorized visitor—can sit at your desk and:
- Read your confidential emails.
- Delete database tables under your username.
- Send offensive or fraudulent messages from your corporate Slack account.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│            THE INSTANT SCREEN LOCK SHORTCUTS           │
│                                                        │
│   🪟 Windows PCs:   **Windows Key + L**                │
│   🍎 Apple Mac:     **Control + Command + Q**          │
│   🐧 Linux (GNOME): **Super Key + L**                  │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

<Callout type="exam">
**The Screen Timeout Standard:** In addition to manual locking, enterprise Group Policies enforce an **Automated Inactivity Screen Lock** set to **5 to 10 minutes**. If a user forgets to lock their machine, the screen automatically blacks out and requires their password/MFA to resume.
</Callout>

## 3. Clean Desk and Clean Screen Policies

- **Clean Desk Policy:** At the end of the workday, all paper documents containing **Personally Identifiable Information (PII)**, employee salaries, student records, or customer credit card details must be locked inside a filing cabinet or shredded.
- **The Sticky Note Hazard:** Writing a password on a sticky note glued to a monitor completely destroys password complexity policies. An attacker walking through the office with a smartphone camera can steal 10 administrative passwords in 30 seconds.
- **Clean Whiteboard Rule:** When a team finishes a brainstorming session in a conference room involving sensitive software architecture or customer data, the whiteboard must be erased before leaving the room.

---

## 4. BYOD (Bring Your Own Device) and USB Safety

When companies allow employees to use personal iPhones or Android devices for work (**BYOD**), it creates a boundary challenge:
- IT deploys **MDM (Mobile Device Management)** software that creates a secure, encrypted **Corporate Container** on the phone.
- Corporate emails and customer contacts live inside the encrypted container, isolated from personal games, TikTok, and personal photos.
- If the employee resigns, IT executes an **Enterprise Selective Wipe**—deleting only the corporate container while leaving the employee's personal family photos untouched.

---

## Why This Matters in the Field

Physical and operational hygiene separates amateur computer users from professional IT technicians.

Developing the instinctive habit of locking your workstation, shredding sensitive printouts, and enforcing the Acceptable Use Policy protects both your organization and your professional reputation.
