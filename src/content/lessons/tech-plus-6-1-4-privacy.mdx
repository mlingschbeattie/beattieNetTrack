## Who Owns Your Digital Identity?

Every time you browse the web, register for an online class, download a mobile app, or swipe a loyalty card at a grocery store, you leave a trail of digital breadcrumbs.

Companies track your search history, log your GPS locations, catalog your purchase habits, and record your biometric face scans.

While cybersecurity focuses on keeping hackers out of servers, **Data Privacy** focuses on a fundamental human question: **What rights do individuals have over their own personal information?**

Understanding data privacy laws, data classification, and responsible data governance is essential for every IT technician.

## 1. Personally Identifiable Information (PII)

In IT, any data that can trace, identify, or contact a specific human being is classified as **PII (Personally Identifiable Information)**:

### Direct PII (Instantly Identifies a Person)
- Full Legal Name
- Social Security Number (SSN) / Passport Number
- Biometric Data (Fingerprint hashes, Retina scans, Facial geometry)
- Financial Account / Credit Card Numbers

### Indirect PII (Identifies When Combined)
A single data point like a ZIP code (`15237`) does not identify a specific person. But research by computer scientists proved that combining just **three data points—5-digit ZIP code, Date of Birth, and Gender—uniquely identifies 87% of the entire United States population!**

<Callout type="exam">
**The FERPA Mandate for Educational IT:** Under the **Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA)**, educational institutions cannot disclose student grades, test performance, or behavioral records to unauthorized third parties without written parental/student consent. Posting student exam grades on a public hallway bulletin board with student names is a direct federal FERPA violation!

## 3. Best Practices: The Data Minimization Principle

The most secure data in the world is the data you **never collected in the first place**:
- **Data Minimization:** Collect only the exact fields necessary to deliver a service. If a mobile flashlight app requests access to your contacts, camera, and GPS location, it is violating data minimization.
- **Purpose Limitation:** If a customer gives you their email address to receive an invoice receipt, you cannot secretly add them to a marketing ad campaign or sell their email to a data broker.
- **Retention Limits & Secure Destruction:** When a student graduates or a customer closes their account, company policies must automatically purge and overwrite their records from active databases and backup tapes.

When you deploy a new help-desk system, configure cloud storage buckets, or manage student accounts at Beattie, you are the custodian of human privacy.
