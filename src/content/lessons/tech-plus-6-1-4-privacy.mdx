## Privacy

Privacy is related to confidentiality but focuses on the **rights of individuals** to control how their personal information is collected, used, and shared. Where confidentiality is a security control, privacy is a legal and ethical obligation.

### Privacy vs. Confidentiality

| | Confidentiality | Privacy |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | Protecting data from unauthorized access | Controlling use of personal information |
| Who it protects | An organization's data | Individuals and their personal data |
| Enforced by | Technical controls | Laws, regulations, policies |
| Concern | Can attackers see this? | Should we collect this? How do we use it? |

### Personally Identifiable Information (PII)

PII is any data that can be used to identify, contact, or locate a specific individual.

**Direct PII:**
- Full name combined with SSN, date of birth, or address
- Passport or driver's license number
- Financial account numbers
- Medical record numbers
- Biometric data (fingerprints, facial recognition)

**Indirect PII (can identify when combined):**
- IP address + browsing history
- ZIP code + birth date + gender (combinable to identify most people)
- Device identifiers

### Privacy Regulations

IT professionals must be aware of applicable laws:

| Regulation | Scope | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| **FERPA** | United States — Education | Student educational records; schools must get consent before sharing |
| **HIPAA** | United States — Healthcare | Protected Health Information (PHI); healthcare providers and partners |
| **COPPA** | United States — Children | Online data collection from children under 13 |
| **GDPR** | European Union | Any org handling EU residents' data; right to access, erasure, portability |
| **CCPA** | California | California residents; right to know, delete, opt-out of data sale |

### Privacy Best Practices for IT Professionals

- **Data minimization** — collect only what is necessary for the purpose
- **Purpose limitation** — don't use data for purposes beyond what was disclosed
- **Retention limits** — don't keep personal data longer than needed; have a deletion schedule
- **Encryption at rest and in transit** — protect PII even from internal exposure
- **Access controls** — limit who can query systems containing PII
- **Breach notification** — most regulations require notifying affected individuals within a defined window (72 hours under GDPR)

### OWASP Connection

**Identification and Authentication Failures** (OWASP #7) and **Security Misconfiguration** (OWASP #5) commonly expose PII — weak passwords let attackers in, and misconfigured storage buckets have leaked millions of records publicly.

### Key Exam Points

- Privacy = individual's right to control their personal data; confidentiality = keeping data from unauthorized access
- **PII** = any data that can identify a specific person
- **FERPA** protects student records; **HIPAA** protects health information
- **GDPR** applies to any organization handling EU residents' data, regardless of where the org is located
- Data minimization: collect only what is needed; retain only as long as necessary
