## Keeping Secrets in a Connected World

Every computer system handles sensitive information: medical diagnoses, student grade transcripts, corporate intellectual property, credit card numbers, and employee passwords.

If that data is exposed to unauthorized individuals, the consequences are devastating: identity theft, massive corporate fines, destroyed reputations, and national security breaches.

**Confidentiality** is the foundational "C" in cybersecurity's **CIA Triad** (Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability). It is the assurance that information is shielded from unauthorized observation at all times—whether resting on an NVMe SSD, traveling across a fiber optic cable, or displayed on an office monitor.

## 1. Threats Against Confidentiality

Attackers use both high-tech network exploits and low-tech physical social engineering to bypass confidentiality:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                      CONFIDENTIALITY THREAT TAXONOMY                   │
├────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Network Sniffing**│ Capturing unencrypted network packets (HTTP, FTP, │
│                    │ Telnet) using packet analyzers (Wireshark).       │
├────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Man-in-the-Middle**│ Attacker silently sits between a laptop and router│
│ **(MitM)**         │ intercepting and reading session tokens.          │
├────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Shoulder Surfing**│ Physically watching an employee type their password│
│                    │ or reading sensitive records over their shoulder. │
├────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Dumpster Diving**│ Searching company trash bins for discarded paper  │
│                    │ invoices, customer forms, or discarded SSDs.      │
├────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Credential Leaks**│ Breaching a weak database and dumping unhashed    │
│                    │ passwords to public hacker forums.                │
└────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 2. Technical Controls: Locking Down Data

To defend confidentiality, IT teams implement layered defense-in-depth:

1. **Encryption at Rest:** Scrambling data on physical drives using **Full Disk Encryption (BitLocker, FileVault)**. If a laptop is stolen from an employee's car, the thief cannot read the files without the cryptographic decryption key.
2. **Encryption in Transit:** Scrambling data traveling over networks using **HTTPS (TLS 1.3), SSH, and IPsec VPNs**. Packet sniffers only see unreadable ciphertext garbage.
3. **Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA):** Requiring a password PLUS a physical hardware token or biometric scan, ensuring a stolen password alone is useless to an attacker.

<Callout type="warn">
**The Plaintext Transmission Trap:** Never transmit passwords, medical data, or financial records over legacy plaintext protocols like **HTTP (Port 80), Telnet (Port 23), or FTP (Port 21)**. Anyone on the local Wi-Fi can capture your plaintext password in 2 seconds!
</Callout>

## 3. Administrative Policies: Least Privilege and Need-to-Know

Technical encryption tools are only half the battle. Administrative policies ensure internal employees don't snoop on data they shouldn't see:

- **Principle of Least Privilege:** Every user and application is granted the **bare minimum permissions necessary** to perform their job duties. A warehouse packer needs access to shipping labels, not executive salary records.
- **Need-to-Know:** Even if an employee holds a high-level security clearance, they are denied access to a sensitive project unless their current assignment specifically requires it.
- **Data Classification:** Labeling corporate documents so employees know how to handle them:
  - **Public:** Marketing brochures, public website text.
  - **Internal:** Company phone directories, cafeteria menus.
  - **Confidential:** Quarterly financial drafts, client contact lists.
  - **Restricted / Top Secret:** Encryption keys, proprietary source code, classified military intelligence.

---

## Why This Matters in the Field

When you deploy a new file share or configure permissions on a cloud folder, a single misconfigured checkbox can expose an entire company's confidential customer database to the public internet.

Practicing the Principle of Least Privilege and enforcing encryption ensures that sensitive data stays in authorized hands.
