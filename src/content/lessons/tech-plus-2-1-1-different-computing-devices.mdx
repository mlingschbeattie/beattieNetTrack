## Different Computing Devices

Modern computing spans a wide spectrum of form factors — from servers humming in data centers to smartwatches on students' wrists. Knowing which device fits which use case is a core Infrastructure skill on the FC0-U71 exam.

### Device Categories

**Desktops** — Stationary systems built for performance and upgradeability. Separate tower, monitor, keyboard, and mouse. Best where raw power and longevity matter.

**Laptops** — Portable all-in-one systems. Battery-powered, integrated display and keyboard. Trade-off: limited upgradability vs. portability.

**Tablets** — Touch-first, lightweight, and often ARM-based. Ideal for content consumption and light productivity. iOS (iPad) and Android are dominant platforms.

**Smartphones** — Pocket-sized computers running full mobile OSes. Always-connected via cellular and Wi-Fi. Consolidate phone, camera, GPS, and internet.

**Wearables** — Smartwatches, fitness trackers. Companion devices that sync with smartphones. Limited independent compute; focus on sensors and notifications.

**Thin Clients** — Minimal local hardware; display and input only. Processing happens on a remote server. Common in enterprise VDI environments.

**Single-Board Computers (SBC)** — Raspberry Pi and similar. Full Linux computer on one board. Used for labs, IoT projects, and education.

**Game Consoles** — Purpose-built for gaming. Increasingly multipurpose (streaming, browsing). Closed ecosystem vs. open PC.

**Servers** — Rack-mounted or tower systems designed for 24/7 uptime, multiple CPUs, ECC RAM, and redundant storage. Not typically used by end users directly.

### Key Exam Points

- Form factor determines **portability vs. performance** trade-off
- Mobile devices (phones, tablets) typically use **ARM processors**; desktops/laptops use **x86/x64**
- Thin clients depend on **network connectivity** — no network = no compute
- IoT and embedded devices often run **real-time operating systems (RTOS)** or stripped Linux
