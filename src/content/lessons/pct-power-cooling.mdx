## Power and Heat: The Two Realities of Every Computer

Every calculation performed by a CPU or GPU requires electricity, and every watt of electricity consumed is ultimately converted into heat. If a computer does not receive clean, stable power, it crashes, corrupts data, or fails to boot. If a computer cannot dissipate the heat it generates, it throttles performance or shuts down automatically to prevent physical destruction.

As a PC technician, you will spend significant time sizing power supplies, diagnosing failing power rails, replacing thermal paste, configuring fan curves, and designing airflow paths. Understanding power and cooling separates technicians who guess from technicians who fix problems permanently.

## Power Supply Units (PSUs)

The Power Supply Unit (PSU) converts alternating current (AC) high-voltage wall power (120V in North America, 230V in Europe) into low-voltage direct current (DC) that sensitive silicon components require.

### The Voltage Rails

Modern ATX power supplies supply three primary DC voltage rails to the motherboard and internal components:

- **+12V Rail (The Heavy Lifter):** Supplies the vast majority of total system power. Powers high-draw components including the CPU (via EPS connector), dedicated graphics card (via PCIe power connectors), cooling pumps, and case fans.
- **+5V Rail:** Supplies power to SATA storage drives, legacy optical drives, USB ports, and onboard motherboard circuitry.
- **+3.3V Rail:** Supplies low-voltage power directly to system RAM (DDR4/DDR5), M.2 NVMe SSD slots, and PCI Express expansion slots.
- **+5VSB (Standby):** Provides a continuous 5V current even when the computer is powered down, enabling features like Wake-on-LAN, power button sensing, and USB charging while sleeping.
- **-12V Rail:** Legacy rail used by older serial ports and specialized audio circuits; carries minimal current.

> [!WARNING]
> **Technician Safety Rule:** Never open a power supply case. Power supplies contain large electrolytic capacitors that can store lethal electrical charges for days or weeks after being disconnected from wall power. If a power supply fails or its internal fan seizes, replace the entire unit.

### PSU Form Factors and Cabling

- **Non-Modular:** All power cables are permanently soldered to the internal PCB. Unused cables must be tucked away inside the case, obstructing airflow and creating clutter.
- **Semi-Modular:** Essential cables (24-pin ATX main motherboard and 8-pin CPU EPS) are fixed; auxiliary SATA, Molex, and PCIe cables are detachable.
- **Fully Modular:** Every single cable is detachable. Technicians only plug in the cables strictly needed for the build, maximizing internal airflow and simplifying cable routing.

### Key Power Connectors

| Connector | Pins | Voltage Delivered | Primary Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ATX 24-Pin Main** | 20+4 pin | +3.3V, +5V, +12V, -12V | Motherboard chipset, PCIe slots, RAM |
| **EPS / ATX12V** | 4-pin or 8-pin (4+4) | +12V | Dedicated CPU Voltage Regulator Module (VRM) |
| **PCIe Power** | 6-pin or 8-pin (6+2) | +12V | Dedicated Graphics Cards (GPUs) |
| **12VHPWR (PCIe 5.0)** | 16-pin (12+4) | +12V (up to 600W) | Modern high-end GPUs |
| **SATA Power** | 15-pin | +3.3V, +5V, +12V | 2.5"/3.5" HDDs, SSDs, optical drives |
| **Molex (4-Pin Peripheral)** | 4-pin | +5V, +12V | Legacy drives, fan hubs, pump controllers |

### Sizing a Power Supply

When selecting a PSU for a build or upgrade, calculate the Thermal Design Power (TDP) of all components (especially CPU and GPU) and add a **20% to 30% headroom buffer**. 

For example:
- CPU Draw: 125W
- GPU Draw: 250W
- Motherboard, RAM, Storage, Fans: 75W
- **Total Estimated Draw:** 450W
- **Headroom Buffer (30%):** 135W
- **Recommended PSU Rating:** 600W to 650W

Why not match wattage exactly? Power supplies achieve their highest electrical efficiency and lowest operating temperatures when running between **50% and 80% of their rated capacity**. Running a PSU near 100% capacity produces excessive heat, increases fan noise, and shortens component lifespan.

### 80 PLUS Efficiency Certification

The 80 PLUS standard certifies that a power supply converts at least 80% of AC wall power into usable DC power across various load levels (20%, 50%, 100%), with the remainder lost as waste heat.

The certification tiers from lowest to highest efficiency:
1. **80 PLUS (White / Standard)** — 80% efficiency
2. **80 PLUS Bronze** — 82% to 85% efficiency
3. **80 PLUS Silver** — 85% to 88% efficiency
4. **80 PLUS Gold** — 87% to 90% efficiency
5. **80 PLUS Platinum** — 89% to 92% efficiency
6. **80 PLUS Titanium** — 90% to 94% efficiency

Higher efficiency means less electricity wasted as heat, lower operating temperatures, quieter fan operation, and reduced long-term power bills.

## Thermal Management and Cooling

Silicon semiconductors generate intense heat in microscopic spaces. If that heat is not transferred away immediately, transistors degrade or permanently fuse.

### Heat Transfer Basics

Heat moves in three ways inside a computer:
1. **Conduction:** Heat transfers directly through physical contact from the CPU die/IHS (Integrated Heat Spreader) to the copper/aluminum base of the cooler.
2. **Convection:** Air or liquid carries heat away from hot heatsink fins or radiator tubes into the surrounding environment.
3. **Thermal Interface Material (TIM / Thermal Paste):** Metal surfaces on the CPU IHS and cooler base look smooth to the human eye, but contain microscopic grooves and pits. Air trapped in these pits acts as an insulator. Thermal paste fills these microscopic voids to provide a continuous conductive bridge.

### Applying Thermal Paste

- **Clean First:** Always clean old thermal paste off the CPU and cooler using 90%+ Isopropyl Alcohol (IPA) and a lint-free microfiber cloth.
- **Amount:** Apply a **pea-sized or rice grain-sized dot** directly in the center of the CPU IHS.
- **Even Pressure:** When mounting the heatsink, tighten screws in an alternating diagonal "X" pattern to spread the paste evenly across the surface.
- **Avoid Excess:** Applying too much thermal paste causes it to squeeze out over the sides of the CPU socket, creating mess and potential short circuits if the paste is electrically conductive.

### Air Cooling vs. Liquid Cooling

| Feature | Air Cooling (Heatsink & Fan) | Liquid Cooling (AIO Closed Loop) |
|---|---|---|
| **Mechanism** | Copper heat pipes transfer heat to aluminum fin stacks; fan blows air through fins. | Liquid absorbs heat at the CPU water block/pump, travels through tubes to a radiator. |
| **Reliability** | Extremely high (only moving part is the fan). | Moderate (pump can fail, fluid can permeate over years). |
| **Noise Profile** | Quiet at idle; fan ramps up under heavy load. | Constant subtle pump hum; radiator fans manage sustained high loads quietly. |
| **Space Constraints** | Tall tower coolers require wide cases and clearance over RAM modules. | Radiator requires dedicated fan mounting slots (120mm, 240mm, 280mm, 360mm). |
| **Best For** | Standard workstations, budget builds, servers requiring maximum uptime. | High-TDP processors, compact cases with restricted vertical clearance, overclocking. |

## Case Airflow Dynamics

A powerful CPU cooler is useless if trapped inside a sealed box of hot air. Technicians must design a coherent airflow path through the chassis.

### Fan Direction and Orientation

Case fans almost always blow air **toward the side with the plastic frame and motor label**.
- Open front face with blades exposed = **Intake side** (air enters here).
- Plastic crossbars and manufacturer sticker = **Exhaust side** (air exits here).
- Most modern fans also feature small direction arrows molded into the side of the plastic housing.

### Standard Airflow Design

1. **Front and Bottom Fans:** Configured as **Intake** (pulling cool ambient room air into the case).
2. **Rear and Top Fans:** Configured as **Exhaust** (expelling hot air rising from the GPU and CPU).

### Pressure Models

- **Positive Pressure (Recommended):** Intake CFM > Exhaust CFM. More air is pushed into the chassis than is pulled out. Air naturally vents out through unsealed seams and vents. When intake fans are covered by dust filters, positive pressure keeps the inside of the case completely free of dust buildup.
- **Negative Pressure:** Exhaust CFM > Intake CFM. Air is sucked into the case through every unfiltered crevice, gap, and port, accelerating dust accumulation inside components.
- **Neutral / Balanced Pressure:** Intake CFM = Exhaust CFM. Optimal thermal throughput, but difficult to balance precisely in practice.

## Thermal Throttling and Diagnostics

Modern CPUs and GPUs incorporate internal thermal sensors that continuously monitor junction temperatures.

- **Thermal Throttling (Dynamic Frequency Scaling):** When a processor reaches its thermal ceiling (typically 90°C to 100°C), it automatically downclocks its frequency and reduces core voltage to prevent damage. Users experience this as sudden frame drops, sluggish UI responsiveness, or stuttering during intensive tasks.
- **Thermal Shutdown:** If temperatures exceed critical safety limits (typically 105°C+), the motherboard triggers an immediate hard power cut to prevent permanent silicon death.
- **Common Overheating Causes:**
  1. Dried or improperly applied thermal paste.
  2. Plastic protective peel left on cooler base during assembly.
  3. Cooler mounting bracket loose or unevenly tensioned.
  4. Dust accumulation blocking heatsink fins or radiator mesh.
  5. AIO pump failure (detected by checking pump RPM in BIOS or listening for pump rattle/silence).
  6. Fans spinning backwards against the case airflow direction.
