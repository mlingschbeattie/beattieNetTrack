## Security Awareness

The most sophisticated firewall in the world can be bypassed by a user who clicks the wrong link. **Security awareness** is the recognition that people are often the weakest link in a security system — and that training and culture are the controls that address it.

### The Human Element

**Social engineering** is the manipulation of people (rather than technical systems) into performing actions or revealing information. Social engineers exploit:
- **Trust** — impersonating authority figures (IT support, executives)
- **Urgency** — "Your account will be suspended in 24 hours!"
- **Fear** — "We detected suspicious activity on your account"
- **Curiosity** — "See who viewed your profile"
- **Helpfulness** — people naturally want to assist

### Common Social Engineering Attacks

| Attack | Description |
|---|---|
| **Phishing** | Fraudulent emails that appear legitimate; trick users into clicking links or entering credentials |
| **Spear phishing** | Targeted phishing using personal details to increase credibility |
| **Whaling** | Phishing targeting executives (CEO, CFO) |
| **Vishing** | Voice phishing — phone calls impersonating banks, IRS, IT support |
| **Smishing** | SMS phishing — malicious text messages |
| **Pretexting** | Creating a fabricated scenario to gain trust (e.g., pretending to be HR) |
| **Baiting** | Leaving infected USB drives in parking lots hoping someone picks them up |
| **Tailgating / Piggybacking** | Following an authorized person through a secure door |
| **Quid pro quo** | Offering something (fake tech support) in exchange for credentials |

### Security Awareness Training Topics

Effective security awareness programs cover:
- How to identify phishing emails (check sender address, hover over links, look for urgency)
- Password hygiene and the importance of MFA
- Physical security (clean desk, screen locking, not letting strangers into secure areas)
- Incident reporting procedures ("if you see something, say something")
- Acceptable use of company devices and networks
- Data handling — what can be emailed, printed, or stored in cloud services

### Red Flags for Phishing

- Sender address doesn't match the organization's domain
- Unexpected urgency or threats
- Links that hover to a different domain than displayed
- Requests for credentials, payment, or sensitive information via email
- Generic greetings ("Dear Customer") instead of your name
- Poor grammar or spelling (though AI has made phishing more convincing)

### Key Exam Points

- Security awareness training addresses the **human element** of security
- **Social engineering** exploits psychology, not technology
- **Phishing** is the most common social engineering attack
- **Spear phishing** is targeted; **whaling** targets executives
- **Tailgating** = following someone through a secure door without credential verification
- Reporting suspicious activity is a key training outcome
