## Bandwidth, Throughput, and Latency

When an end user calls the help desk complaining that *"the network is slow,"* you have to figure out what "slow" actually means. Are large files taking forever to download? Or is there a half-second delay every time they click a button on a web app?

These are two completely different networking problems governed by three distinct metrics:

1. **Bandwidth (The Width of the Pipe):** The maximum theoretical data capacity of a transmission medium, measured in bits per second (bps, Mbps, Gbps). A 1 Gbps fiber line has 10x the bandwidth of a 100 Mbps copper line.
2. **Throughput (The Actual Flow):** The amount of actual payload data delivered across the network per second after subtracting protocol overhead (TCP/IP headers), encryption encapsulation, radio interference, and network collisions. Throughput is always lower than theoretical bandwidth.
3. **Latency (The Delay / Ping):** The time it takes for a data packet to travel from the source to the destination and back, measured in **milliseconds (ms)**.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                     THE HIGHWAY TRAFFIC ANALOGY                        │
├────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Metric     │ Highway Comparison                                        │
├────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Bandwidth  │ The number of lanes on the highway (e.g., 8-lane freeway) │
│ Throughput │ How many cars actually pass the toll booth per minute     │
│ Latency    │ The speed limit / how long it takes 1 car to reach exit   │
└────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

<Callout type="warn">
**The Satellite Internet Trap:** Geostationary satellite internet (like legacy HughesNet) might offer a decent 50 Mbps bandwidth. But because the radio signal must travel 22,000 miles into space and back, the **latency is 600–800 ms**. Streaming a movie will work fine (buffering absorbs the delay), but playing an online game or making a Zoom call will feel miserably laggy.
</Callout>

## Network Interface Speeds: Ethernet and Wi-Fi

Every network cable and wireless access point operates under standardized speed classifications:

### Wired Ethernet Standards (IEEE 802.3)
- **Fast Ethernet:** 100 Mbps (Legacy standard; mostly found on cheap printers or legacy IoT hubs)
- **Gigabit Ethernet (1000BASE-T):** 1,000 Mbps = 1 Gbps (The universal modern baseline for desktop PCs, game consoles, and enterprise switches)
- **2.5G / 5GBASE-T:** 2.5 Gbps / 5 Gbps (Intermediate standard designed to run over existing Cat5e/Cat6 cabling for high-end Wi-Fi 6 access points and NAS drives)
- **10 Gigabit Ethernet (10GBASE-T / SFP+):** 10 Gbps (Server backbones, datacenter switches, and enterprise virtualization storage networks)

### Wi-Fi Standards (IEEE 802.11)
Wi-Fi marketing uses consumer-friendly generation names to simplify IEEE technical alphabet soup:

| Generation | IEEE Standard | Frequency Bands | Max Theoretical Speed | Common Use |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|---|
| **Wi-Fi 4** | 802.11n | 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz | 600 Mbps | Older laptops, smart home plugs, budget devices |
| **Wi-Fi 5** | 802.11ac | 5 GHz | 3.5 Gbps | Widespread consumer routers and smartphones |
| **Wi-Fi 6 / 6E** | 802.11ax | 2.4, 5, and **6 GHz** | 9.6 Gbps | Enterprise high-density offices, flagship phones |
| **Wi-Fi 7** | 802.11be | 2.4, 5, and 6 GHz | 46 Gbps | Next-generation ultra-low latency VR/AR & multi-gigabit wireless |

## Peripheral and Bus Transfer Rates

Data doesn't just travel across networks; it travels across physical motherboard traces, cables, and buses inside the PC:

### Universal Serial Bus (USB)
- **USB 2.0 (Hi-Speed):** 480 Mbps ≈ 60 MB/s (White or black physical ports)
- **USB 3.0 / 3.1 Gen 1 (SuperSpeed 5Gbps):** 5 Gbps ≈ 625 MB/s (Blue physical ports)
- **USB 3.1 Gen 2 (SuperSpeed 10Gbps):** 10 Gbps ≈ 1.25 GB/s (Teal, red, or USB-C ports)
- **USB 3.2 Gen 2x2:** 20 Gbps (Dual-lane USB-C only)
- **USB4 / Thunderbolt 4:** 40 Gbps (High-speed USB-C; connects external GPUs, PCIe storage, and dual 4K monitors)

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                INTERNAL STORAGE BUS SPEEDS             │
├──────────────────────┬─────────────┬───────────────────┤
│ Interface            │ Throughput  │ Drive Technology  │
├──────────────────────┼─────────────┼───────────────────┤
│ SATA III (6 Gbps)    │ ~550 MB/s   │ 2.5" SSDs, HDDs   │
│ M.2 NVMe (PCIe 3.0)  │ ~3,500 MB/s │ Modern SSDs       │
│ M.2 NVMe (PCIe 4.0)  │ ~7,000 MB/s │ PS5, Fast NVMe    │
│ M.2 NVMe (PCIe 5.0)  │ ~14,000 MB/s│ Flagship PC Gen 5 │
└──────────────────────┴─────────────┴───────────────────┘
```

<Callout type="exam">
Notice how M.2 NVMe drives are over **10x to 25x faster** than SATA III SSDs. SATA is throttled by a legacy 6 Gbps bus designed for spinning hard drives. NVMe connects directly to raw PCI Express motherboard lanes.
</Callout>

## The Technician's Download Calculation Formula

To calculate download or transfer times in the field, follow the **2-Step Rule**:

1. **Convert connection speed from Megabits (Mbps) to Megabytes (MB/s):**
   Transfer Speed (MB/s) = (Connection Speed in Mbps / 8)

2. **Divide file size by the transfer speed:**
   Time in Seconds = (File Size in MB / Transfer Speed in MB/s)

#### Real-World Example:
A client is downloading a **12 Gigabyte (12,000 MB)** operating system ISO image on a **240 Mbps** office internet connection. How long will it take?

1. 240 Mbps \div 8 = 30 MB/s
2. 12,000 MB \div 30 MB/s = 400 seconds
3. 400 seconds \div 60 = 6.67 minutes

## Why This Matters in the Field

When you install a 10Gbps Network Interface Card in a server, but plug it into a Cat5e cable running into a Fast Ethernet (100Mbps) switch, the entire connection drops to the speed of the slowest link: 100 Mbps.

A system is only as fast as its narrowest bottleneck. Knowing the exact throughput limits of USB ports, Wi-Fi channels, SATA buses, and Ethernet standards allows you to spot performance bottlenecks instantly.
