  - id: q2

    prompt: "An attacker changes grade values in an administrative database without authorization. Which property is primarily violated?"
    options:
      - "Integrity"
      - "Availability"
      - "Confidentiality"
      - "Accountability"
    correctIndex: 0
    explanation: "Integrity guarantees data remains untampered and accurate. Silently altering database values poisons the integrity of the data."
  - id: q3
    type: single
    prompt: "A ransomware payload encrypts virtual disks, locking staff out of systems during school operations. Which CIA property is most directly affected?"
    options:
      - "Confidentiality"
      - "Availability"
      - "Integrity"
      - "Authenticity"
    correctIndex: 1
    explanation: "Availability means authorized users can reach the systems and data they need. Denying access to operational systems is an availability failure."
  - id: q4
    type: single
    prompt: "A student-records export directory on a Linux server is set to mode 777. Which CIA properties does that setting most directly put at risk?"
    options:
      - "Availability, because 777 makes the directory slower to access"
      - "Confidentiality and integrity — any user or process on the system can read and modify the records"
      - "Integrity only, because 777 does not grant read access"
      - "None, provided the server sits behind a firewall"
    correctIndex: 1
    explanation: "777 grants read, write, and execute to everyone, so any account on the box can read the records and change them. A firewall only filters network traffic — it does nothing about users and processes already on the server."
  - id: q5
    type: single
    prompt: "What best defines a vulnerability in an enterprise environment?"
    options:
      - "Any malicious adversary with an exploit tool"
      - "A flaw or weakness in software, hardware, or policy"
      - "The estimated financial damage after an incident"
      - "A technical control used to block phishing"
    correctIndex: 1
    explanation: "A vulnerability is the passive weakness or hole in the fence. Threat actors exploit vulnerabilities to cause impact."
  - id: q6
    type: single
    prompt: "A student's old gaming account, unused for years, shares a password with their school account. The gaming site is breached. In risk terms, what is the reused password?"
    options:
      - "The threat"
      - "The vulnerability"
      - "The impact"
      - "A risk transfer"
    correctIndex: 1
    explanation: "The attacker holding the leaked list is the threat. The reuse is the weakness they exploit. A taken-over school account would be the impact. The same three-part split from the risk lesson applies to your own accounts."
  - id: q7
    type: single
    prompt: "Which statement accurately describes risk in cybersecurity operations?"
    options:
      - "Risk is identical to threat"
      - "Risk is another name for an unpatched vulnerability"
      - "Risk balances threat likelihood against operational impact"
      - "Risk cannot be calculated or reduced"
    correctIndex: 2
    explanation: "Risk is the product of Threat, Vulnerability, and Impact. It measures how likely an exploit is and how badly it will hurt."
  - id: q8
    type: multi
    prompt: "Select EVERY action below that is risk mitigation rather than transfer, acceptance, or avoidance."
    options:
      - "Enabling MFA on all staff accounts"

      - "Removing Domain Admin rights from a service account that only reads logs"
    correctIndices: [0, 3]
    explanation: "MFA and removing excess privilege both reduce likelihood or impact, so both are mitigation. Insurance is transfer. Shutting the service down is avoidance — the activity creating the risk no longer exists."
  - id: q9

    acceptedAnswers: ["residual", "residual risk"]
    explanation: "Residual risk. No security architecture reaches zero, and what remains has to be consciously accepted, transferred, or mitigated further."
  - id: q10
    type: single
    prompt: "What is the primary goal of a defense-in-depth architecture?"
    options:
      - "Rely on a single enterprise perimeter firewall"
      - "Deploy layered controls so that the failure of one does not collapse overall defense"
      - "Completely eliminate all residual risk from the network"
      - "Replace real-time monitoring with automated prevention"
    correctIndex: 1
    explanation: "Defense in depth layers physical, network, endpoint, and administrative controls so that an attacker who bypasses one layer is still stopped by the next."
  - id: q11
    type: single
    prompt: "A service account configured with Domain Admin privileges when it only reads an inventory log violates which fundamental security concept?"
    options:
      - "Defense in depth"
      - "Attack surface reduction"
      - "Principle of least privilege"
      - "Network microsegmentation"
    correctIndex: 2
    explanation: "Least privilege requires giving accounts only the exact permissions needed to do their job and nothing more. Granting full admin rights to a log reader is an accident waiting to happen."
  - id: q12
    type: single
    prompt: "Which action most directly reduces an organization's attack surface?"
    options:
      - "Enabling all optional services for convenience"
      - "Disabling unused ports, default accounts, and legacy protocols"
      - "Allowing shared administrative accounts across IT staff"
      - "Disabling system logging to conserve disk space"
    correctIndex: 1
    explanation: "Attack surface is the total sum of entry points an attacker can target. Shutting down unused ports and services removes those doors entirely."
  - id: q13
    type: single
    prompt: "Why should a school's IT team prioritise patching, MFA, and closing unused ports over defending against nation-state zero-day exploits?"
    options:
      - "Most real attacks are opportunistic and exploit known, unpatched weaknesses, so basic hygiene stops far more actual incidents"
      - "Nation-state actors never target schools"
      - "Zero-day exploits cannot be defended against in any way"
      - "Firewalls block all advanced persistent threats automatically"
    correctIndex: 0
    explanation: "Attackers take the path of least resistance, and that path is almost always a known, unpatched weakness rather than a novel exploit. Schools are targeted, so the second option is false — and the third overstates it: defense in depth limits what a zero-day achieves even when it cannot be prevented."
  - id: q14
    type: multi
    prompt: "A staff member receives: 'Hi, it's Dana from payroll. We're switching banks today — reply with your new direct deposit details before 3pm or this month's pay will be delayed.' The sender is dana@schoo1-payroll.com. Select EVERY red flag."

  - id: q15

    acceptedAnswers: ["vishing", "voice phishing"]

  - id: q16

      - "A photo the teacher posted of their classroom, showing the whiteboard"
      - "The teacher's encrypted email inbox"
    correctIndices: [0, 1, 2]
    explanation: "Everything except the inbox is published — by the school or by the teacher. That is OSINT, and it is the bridge between the digital footprint lesson and the social engineering lesson: what you post becomes the material for the lure. The inbox would require a compromise first."
  - id: q17
    type: single
    prompt: "A caller knows your principal's name, the district ticketing system, and a real open ticket number, and asks for your MFA code to 'close the ticket'. What do you do?"
    options:

      - "Refuse, end the call, and contact the IT help desk using the number from the district directory"
      - "Give the code, then change your password straight afterwards"
      - "Ask them to send the request by email instead"
    correctIndex: 1
    explanation: "Accurate detail can come from OSINT or from an attacker who already has a foothold, so it proves nothing. No legitimate IT process needs your MFA code. Asking for an email changes nothing — it is the same untrusted source on a different channel. Verify out of band, through a number you already trust."

  - id: q19
    type: multi
    prompt: "You suspect a Linux server is compromised. Select EVERY command that gathers information without changing the system."
    options:

      - "find / -perm -4000 2>/dev/null"
    correctIndices: [0, 1, 3]
    explanation: "Listing processes, following the auth log, and searching for SUID binaries are all read-only. chmod changes permissions, and on /tmp it would remove the sticky bit and make things worse. Observe before you change anything."
